## What it is
A one-time-purchase digital pack that takes a buyer from "I have a Claude subscription" to a working AI agent crew — with defined roles, daily/weekly routines, and a first business track launched — in one weekend, with zero programming. The buyer pays Wil ONCE for the copy-paste/config/teaching layer and subscribes to every underlying tool directly (in their own name, ideally via Wil's referral links where a real program exists).

## The honest promise (the only pitch allowed)
"Skip ~20 hours of setup, dead ends, and prompt archaeology. You still run the business — strategy, judgment, approvals. This pack hands you the machine, already assembled and tested."
Banned everywhere in copy: income claims, "passive," "autonomous income," "AI that never says no," screenshots of anyone's revenue. (FTC + Wil's verify-every-claim rule. Isaac Blake's Polsia scripts are the anti-template.)

## The wedge (vs Polsia and the platform crowd)
Polsia: $49/mo + 3% of every customer payment + 20% of ad spend, forever, on a platform where ~10% of companies ever made $1 (founder's own number).
Operator Loadout: **pay once, own your stack, subscribe direct, keep 100% of your revenue.** The crew runs on the buyer's own Claude account — no middleman can raise the rent.

## Who it's for
Side-hustle starters and small operators who already pay (or will pay) for Claude and stalled at "now what do I actually DO with it." Not for: people wanting zero work, people wanting a specific niche pack (those are Loadouts #2/#3), anyone needing the tools' subscriptions included (they're not — see true-cost table).

COMPLIANCE (from the actual FTC v. Hims & Hers complaint, filed July 29, 2026 — FTC + Utah + California; the reel's "only illegal for controlled substances" framing is WRONG): the quiz mechanic is legal; what got Hims sued was (i) charging/enrolling before the promised consultation — consent before any charge, terms unmissable; (ii) obstructive cancellation — cancel must be one obvious path, labeled "cancel"; (iii) sharing sensitive intake data with Meta/Snap ad platforms — quiz answers never go to ad platforms, pixels get configured to exclude intake data. All three rules bind every funnel, pack lesson, and sales page in this business.

## ADDENDUM v2 — THE ACTUAL 3-PROMPT ARCHITECTURE (from the deep extract, 2026-10-21)
The reel's system, recovered (Prompts 2–3 legible on screen; Prompt 1's demo instruction visible):
1. **ARCHETYPES prompt** (Claude + CRM): pull all contacts/deals with activity timelines and won/lost status; mine threads/transcripts into buyer archetypes → save **archetypes.md** (names + pain points + real customer language). His: Hands-Off Owner, DIY Builder, Burned Skeptic, Scaling Agency. For Wil: Flyra (Good Guys) or the buyer's own list/sheet.
2. **QUIZ MAP prompt** (reads archetypes.md): design **6–8 questions** — multiple-choice + **2 open questions**; each answer annotated with the archetype it points to; **2 shared questions first, then per-archetype branches** built from pain points; questions worded the way each archetype talks; full map saved to **quiz_map.json** with archetype IDs.
3. **BUILD prompt**: mobile quiz from quiz_map.json (one question/screen, progress bar); after EVERY answer, classify all answers so far against the archetype IDs — route to an archetype's branch when its probability passes **0.85**, else continue shared questions; end on that archetype's landing page (one page per type, written from archetypes.md pain points + quotes); **log every answer with a user ID** to find which questions screen people out.
Classifier options: his build uses "Jev" (TypeSafe, api.typesafe.ai, API key required) — a third-party vendor receiving every answer. Default for Wil's builds: **Claude-native classification** (no new vendor, no extra data holder); Jev noted as the alternative if latency/cost demands it. Quiz topics stay non-sensitive by design.
