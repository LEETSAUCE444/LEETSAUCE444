## Day 4 · Sun Oct 25 · Field: finishing (reel: "Starting is your superpower. Finishing is the skill.")
1. Almost finished is still unfinished.
2. The last 10% is the part customers can see.
3. Nobody claps for a project you almost shipped.
4. Finish small before you finish big.
5. Finished teaches you something. Unfinished just sits there.
6. Done is a decision you make before you feel ready.

## Day 5 · Mon Oct 26 · Field: shipping (reel: "It pays for what you shipped.")
1. The feedback starts the day it ships.
2. Shipped beats perfect, every single week.
3. A small launch is still a launch.
4. Version one is supposed to be a little embarrassing.
5. Ship it Friday. Improve it Monday.
6. Post it. Price it. Let the market answer.

## Day 6 · Tue Oct 27 · Field: focus / starting (reel: "Start for 10 minutes and let focus catch up.")
1. Focus is a fence, not a feeling.
2. Guard your first hour. The rest of the day can wait.
3. Single-tasking feels slow. It isn't.
4. Start ugly. Adjust in motion.
5. Your phone is not a break. It's a leak.
6. One task, one timer, no tabs.

## Day 7 · Wed Oct 28 · Field: consistency (reel: "One week in. None felt ready. All of them went out.")
Post only under the same condition as the Day 7 reel (Days 1–7 all went out; otherwise hold this set for the make-up day).
1. Seven days of imperfect beats one perfect someday.
2. Keep the bar low enough to clear every day.
3. You don't need a bigger launch. You need a longer streak.
4. The days nobody watches count the most.
5. Consistency is boring on purpose.
6. Week one is data, not a verdict.

## Day 8 · Thu Oct 29 · Field: capture it / write it down (reel: "Your head is a terrible filing cabinet.")
1. Write it messy. Nobody grades the notes.
2. A notebook never says "wait, what was that idea?"
3. Capturing an idea isn't committing to it.
4. Read your notes every Friday. The good ones hide there.
5. The idea you write down today is tomorrow's head start.
6. Voice memo it now. Sort it tonight.

## Day 9 · Fri Oct 30 · Field: AI as your team (reel: "AI replaces the excuse 'I don't have a team.'")
1. You don't need a bigger team. You need the work divided.
2. Let AI draft the research. You handle the relationships.
3. A solo founder with systems isn't really solo.
4. Check AI's work like you'd check a new hire's.
5. Teach it once with an example. Skip the long explanation.
6. Give AI a job description, not a wish.
