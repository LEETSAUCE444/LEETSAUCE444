## The system — a 3-PROMPT pipeline (Prompts 2 and 3 substantially legible on screen)
**Prompt 1 — ARCHETYPES (Claude Desktop, model shown: "Opus 5").** Pull every contact and deal from HubSpot with full activity timeline and won/lost status; match email threads and call transcripts into buyer archetypes; save to **archetypes.md**. His demo output: **Hands-Off Owner · DIY Builder · Burned Skeptic · Scaling Agency**. (Definitions live inside archetypes.md — never shown in the reel; gated behind the DM funnel.)
**Prompt 2 — QUIZ MAP** (same project; reads archetypes.md). Design **6–8 questions** that identify the archetype: mix multiple-choice with **2 open questions**; annotate which archetype each answer points to; start with **2 shared questions**, then branch into per-archetype follow-ups built from each type's pain points; **word the questions the way each archetype talks**; draw the full map (every question, branch, landing page); save to **quiz_map.json** using archetype IDs.
**Prompt 3 — BUILD THE FUNNEL.** Build a mobile quiz from quiz_map.json — one question per screen, progress bar. After EVERY answer, send all answers so far to a classifier ("Jev," api.typesafe.ai — needs a TypeSafe API key) as a single multiple-choice question over the archetype IDs; when the top archetype's probability passes **0.85**, switch to that archetype's branch; otherwise continue with shared questions. (His on-screen demo: 77% after answer 1, 100% after answer 2.) End on the matched archetype's landing page — **one page per archetype, written from its pain points and real quotes in archetypes.md**. Two build rules in his own prompt worth stealing verbatim-in-spirit: **show the price and exactly what they're signing up for before submit**, and **log every answer with a user ID** so you can see which questions screen people out.

## The legal framing — corrected (verified against reporting + the court doc he flashes)
On screen: the actual complaint — N.D. Cal., **Case 3:26-cv-03871, filed 07/29/26**, FTC + the People of California (LA County Counsel) v. Hims & Hers Health, Inc.; Utah's Division of Consumer Protection is also a co-plaintiff per legal-press summaries (JD Supra: "FTC, joined by Utah and California").
His spoken frame: Hims got sued "because the quizzes were way too effective," and quiz funnels are "totally chill — only illegal when you get them to buy controlled substances right after" (illustrated with a Hers anxiety/depression checkout: 3-Month Starter Plan, $147 every 3 months, "Due Now $0 / Pay $0 today").
THE CORRECTION (already delivered to Wil at ID time): the complaint's clusters are (1) **deceptive subscription billing** — charging/enrolling almost immediately after the intake form despite promising a provider consultation first (FTC Act §5 + ROSCA); one cited customer charged $897 pre-consultation; (2) **obstructive cancellation** — cancel buried behind "add/remove items from order," the word "cancel" never used; (3) **sensitive health data shared with Meta/Snap** ad platforms (customer lists + tracking tech) despite "100% online, private, and secure" promises. The quiz is the front door, not the defendant. Irony noted: the Hers checkout he jokes with ("Pay $0 today," real charge later) is itself the billing pattern cluster (1) attacks — while his own Prompt 3 quietly contains the compliant rule (show price + exactly what's being bought BEFORE submit).
Utah relevance: Wil's own state is an active enforcer on exactly this fact pattern, and his business runs recurring plans — the three clusters are a negative template for Good Guys' plan billing (his model — clear ladder, cancel anytime, consented card-on-file — is the compliant opposite).

## The classifier dependency (new flag from the deep read)
"Jev" (TypeSafe, api.typesafe.ai) is a third-party API: every quiz answer is sent to it after every question. For ordinary marketing quizzes that's a normal SaaS dependency (key + per-call cost, unverified pricing), BUT: (a) it's one more vendor holding prospect data — for Wil's builds, a Claude call can do the same classification with no new vendor, at the cost of latency/tokens; the block/spec now note both routes; (b) if a quiz EVER touched sensitive topics, routing answers to a third-party classifier + ad pixels is precisely the Hims fact pattern — Wil's quizzes stay non-sensitive by design (business fit, home size, product concerns).
